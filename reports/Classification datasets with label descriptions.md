# Scarce intent descriptions force a hybrid training pool

Only two public intent corpora ship official per-label descriptions. **Schema-Guided Dialogue (SGD)** has 88 service-intent pairs with human-written descriptions of about 8 words, five crowd-paraphrased variants of each in SGD-X, and a CC-BY-SA-4.0 license. **NLU++** has 62 banking and hotel intents, each described as a yes/no question, under CC-BY-4.0. Every other mainstream intent benchmark (Banking77, CLINC150, HWU64, MASSIVE, Bitext, Redwood, MINDS-14, SNIPS, ATIS) ships label names only. A late-interaction reader for short-message intents therefore has to combine those two sources with three others: taxonomies that can be joined to official definitions (MeSH, ESCO, O*NET, IPTC Media Topics), tool catalogs whose options are already "name: description" (MetaTool, ToolBench, ToolRet), and descriptions it writes for name-only intent sets. The published evidence supports the "name: description" format, with a condition. Descriptions add **17-19 accuracy points when an encoder is trained on them**, **6.3-6.7 joint goal accuracy (JGA, a dialogue-state-tracking metric) points on unseen SGD services**, and about **6 points on unseen CLINC domains when the descriptions contrast sibling intents**. Untrained NLI models given bare dictionary definitions get worse. No paper isolates the effect for a bi-encoder or late-interaction classifier on Banking77 or CLINC150, so a clean LIDM ablation would be new evidence. Leakage is the binding constraint. UTCD, tasksource label-NLI, tasksource-jev, Redwood, RAFT, the AutoIntent re-uploads and Hu et al.'s description release all contain Banking77 or CLINC150 directly, and LIDM's own stage-1 mixture still trains on UTCD's CLINC150 split with only its banking intents removed. Semantic neighbours such as SGD's bank services, NLU++ banking, the Bitext banking sets, MINDS-14 and HWU64/MASSIVE/SLURP are not contamination in the strict sense. They will still inflate "unseen label" scores unless results on them are reported separately.

## Only SGD and NLU++ ship official intent descriptions

**SGD is the richest human-written intent-description source in dialogue data.** It covers over 20k assistant conversations across 20 domains ([SGD README](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/dstc8-schema-guided-dialogue)). Deduplicated across its train, dev and test schema files, it has **45 services, 88 service-intent pairs (46 unique intent names) and 365 slots**. The mean intent description is **8.2 words**, for example `Banks_1.CheckBalance` is "Check the amount of money in a user's bank account" and `Buses_2.FindBus` is "Find a bus itinerary between cities for a given date" ([SGD train schema](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research-datasets/dstc8-schema-guided-dialogue/master/train/schema.json)). SGD-X adds what no other intent resource has: **five independently written variants of every schema, 4,755 paraphrases of names and descriptions by 400+ crowd workers who never saw the originals** ([Lee et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.06800)). The same balance intent becomes `CheckBankBalance`, "See amount available in account", in v1 and `ProvideBankAccountBalance`, "Show available funds in a specified banking account", in v5 ([SGD-X v1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research-datasets/dstc8-schema-guided-dialogue/master/sgd_x/data/v1/train/schema.json), [v5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research-datasets/dstc8-schema-guided-dialogue/master/sgd_x/data/v5/train/schema.json)). Six wordings of 88 intents gives roughly 528 distinct name-and-description strings. That figure is an estimate, because the variants were not counted one by one. There are two caveats. The license is **CC-BY-SA-4.0**, so redistributed derivatives inherit share-alike terms ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google-research-datasets/schema_guided_dstc8)). Labels are also active intents per dialogue frame, not per utterance, so "short message, intent" pairs have to be derived by taking the user turn where the active intent changes. That is a derivation, not an official split.

**NLU++ is the only classic utterance-level benchmark with an official description file.** It has 3,080 examples and 62 intents, of which 48 appear in banking and 40 in hotels with overlap between the two. It is multi-label: composable "intent modules" such as `how_much` plus `transfer_payment_deposit` ([NLU++ README](https://github.com/PolyAI-LDN/task-specific-datasets/tree/master/nlupp)). Its `ontology.json` phrases every intent as a question, for example `handoff` is "is the intent to speak to a human or hand off?" ([ontology.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PolyAI-LDN/task-specific-datasets/master/nlupp/data/ontology.json)). The license is CC-BY-4.0. Because each description is a yes/no question, it maps directly onto LIDM's per-label binary (`noul`) primitive. The banking half sits squarely in Banking77's domain.

Three smaller dialogue resources fill niches. **MultiWOZ 2.2** has only 11 intents with lowercase glosses such as "search for places to wine and dine", under MIT ([schema](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/budzianowski/multiwoz/master/data/MultiWOZ_2.2/schema.json)). **STARv2** has 6,652 dialogues over 24 tasks, with natural-language user and system action descriptions such as "user wants to make a reservation at a restaurant", under Apache-2.0 ([STARv2](https://github.com/google-research/task-oriented-dialogue/tree/main/starv2)). Its descriptions were produced by a D3ST model fine-tuned on a few hand-labelled conversations, so they are model-written annotations ([AnyTOD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09939)). **ABCD** has 10k+ customer-service dialogues and 55 subflows, under MIT. Its "descriptions" are procedural agent guidelines ("All chats in this flow start by asking the customer for their full name…"), so they need summarising before they can serve as options ([ABCD guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/asappresearch/abcd/master/data/guidelines.json)). Unsummarised, they would also overrun LIDM's 64-token option window.

Everything else ships names, plus some community add-ons. DeepPavlov's AutoIntent re-uploads on Hugging Face carry **Qwen3-32B one-sentence descriptions** for CLINC150 (150), HWU64 (64), MASSIVE (58), MINDS-14 (14) and SNIPS (7), in a `intentsqwen3-32b` config, for example "User wants to set an alarm." The cards have no license field ([DeepPavlov/clinc150](https://huggingface.co/datasets/DeepPavlov/clinc150)). The two research notes disagree about Banking77. One found `DeepPavlov/banking77` with null descriptions. The other quoted 77 of 77 filled entries, such as "User wants to activate his new card." ([datasets-server](https://datasets-server.huggingface.co/splits?dataset=DeepPavlov/banking77)). Check this before relying on it. Hu et al. hand-wrote templated "User is asking / User wants…" descriptions for ATIS, SNIPS, CLINC and MASSIVE ([arXiv 2407.17862](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17862)). Both add-on sets target the held-out benchmarks, so they belong in evaluation, not training.

## Taxonomies and tool catalogs supply tens of thousands of described labels

**Beyond dialogue, official taxonomies give the largest volume of definitional label text, but quality varies sharply.** MeSH is the standout. Of its **30,594 descriptors, 99.4% carry a scope note**, averaging 198 characters, such as "Neoplasms: New abnormal growth of tissue…" ([MeSH SPARQL](https://id.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/sparql)). BioASQ's 15.56M PubMed articles use 29,369 of those labels, at 12.68 per article ([BioASQ 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14885)). For short texts, **ESCO** is the strongest fit. Version 1.2 has 3,039 occupations and 13,939 skills with multi-sentence descriptions in 28 languages, and may be reused "for any purpose… free of charge" with attribution ([ESCO FAQ](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/et/about-esco/faq)). TechWolf publishes job-ad sentences labelled with it, including about 138K synthetic skill sentences covering 99.5% of ESCO skills ([TechWolf](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TechWolf/Synthetic-ESCO-skill-sentences)). Watch for version drift: those labels use ESCO v1.0.5 and v1.1.0, while the current descriptions are v1.2.x. **O*NET** describes all 1,016 occupations, at a mean of 214 characters, under a Creative Commons license, but no public labelled corpus was found for it ([O*NET 30.3](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/database/db_30_3_text/Occupation%20Data.txt)). **IPTC Media Topics** is the cleanest small benchmark. All 1,059 active concepts have an English definition (mean 99 characters) under CC BY 4.0, and the MN-DS corpus labels 10,917 news articles with its 17 top-level and 109 second-level topics ([IPTC CV](https://cv.iptc.org/newscodes/mediatopic/?lang=en-GB&format=json); [MN-DS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.12061)). Its top-level definitions are near-tautological ("All forms of arts, entertainment, cultural heritage and media"), so descriptions only add signal at deeper levels.

Other taxonomies disappoint on inspection. **EuroVoc**, the label space of MultiEURLEX (65k EU laws, 23 languages, CC BY 4.0), defines only about 23% of its concepts. MultiEURLEX's level-1 (21) and level-2 (127) labels have **no definitions at all**, and only 25% of its level-3 labels do. Some scope notes are indexing rules such as "Use for the sale of products from machines" ([EuroVoc SPARQL](https://publications.europa.eu/webapi/rdf/sparql); [MultiEURLEX](https://huggingface.co/datasets/coastalcph/multi_eurlex)). **CPC patent definitions** are rich, but each is a multi-section document of thousands of words per subclass ([USPTO G06N](https://www.uspto.gov/web/patents/classification/cpc/html/defG06N.html)). The HUPD patent dataset also carries a CC BY-SA versus CC BY-NC-SA license conflict ([HUPD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HUPD/hupd)). The **DBpedia ontology** comments only 282 of 790 classes, and only 4 of the 14 DBpedia-14 classes ([DBpedia SPARQL](https://dbpedia.org/sparql)). One-line Wikidata glosses are a better substitute ([Wikidata API](https://www.wikidata.org/w/api.php?action=wbgetentities&ids=Q5|Q4830453|Q11424&props=labels|descriptions&languages=en&format=json)). The **arXiv** taxonomy (155 categories) has one-liners, some of which read "Description coming soon" ([arXiv taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/category_taxonomy)). ICD-10-CM codes come with titles rather than definitions, and the main ICD-coded corpus, MIMIC-IV, requires credentialed access ([PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/mimiciv/)). The Google product taxonomy has 5,595 categories with no descriptions ([Google taxonomy](https://www.google.com/basepages/producttype/taxonomy-with-ids.en-US.txt)). The extreme-classification "LF" sets (131K-1.3M labels) bundle only 3-5-word titles and state no license ([XC Repository](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/manikvarma/manikvarma.github.io/master/downloads/XC/XMLRepository.html)). These last three work only as name-only controls.

**Tool-selection data offers the most label variety in a ready-made "name: description" shape.** **MetaTool** is the closest to "short query, pick one option". It has 21,127 queries over 199 tools with one-sentence marketing descriptions ("Planning something outdoors? Get the 2-day air quality forecast…"). Its GitHub repo is MIT, but the paper is listed as CC BY-NC-SA ([MetaTool](https://github.com/HowieHwong/MetaTool); [plugin_des.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HowieHwong/MetaTool/master/dataset/plugin_des.json)). At larger scale:

- **ToolBench**: 16,464 RapidAPI APIs over 3,451 tools, Apache-2.0 ([ToolBench](https://github.com/OpenBMB/ToolBench)).
- **ToolRet**: about 43k tools and 7.6k retrieval tasks ([arXiv 2503.01763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01763v1)).
- **ToolACE**: 26,507 synthetic APIs, Apache-2.0 ([ToolACE](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Team-ACE/ToolACE)).
- **xLAM**: 3,673 APIs in 60k entries, CC-BY-4.0 and gated ([xLAM](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Salesforce/xlam-function-calling-60k)).
- **When2Call**: 3,652 test and 15,000 training items whose tool lists come from BFCL Live, CC-BY-4.0 ([When2Call](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/When2Call)).

Their description styles are marketing copy, noisy RapidAPI text or clean but templated synthetic specs, and their queries are longer and more task-like than banking chat. They are best used to stress-test large candidate sets, not as in-style intent signal.

A handful of other described-label sets have no Banking77 or CLINC150 overlap:

- **FewRel's `pid2name.json`**: 744 Wikidata relations, each with a one-line description, MIT ([FewRel](https://github.com/thunlp/FewRel/blob/master/data/pid2name.json)).
- **GoEmotions**: one-line definitions for 27 emotions over 58,009 Reddit comments ([Demszky et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00547)).
- **Gao et al.'s LabelDesc**: about 417 hand-written terms, definitions and Wikipedia sentences for topic and sentiment labels. The repo has no license ([LabelDescTraining](https://github.com/lingyugao/LabelDescTraining)).
- **Laurer's `synthetic_zeroshot_mixtral_v0.1`**: one sentence-length hypothesis per class over about 500 synthetic tasks, Apache-2.0 ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MoritzLaurer/synthetic_zeroshot_mixtral_v0.1)). LIDM already trains on it.
- **`knowledgator/gliclass-v2.0`**: 1.2M rows with hypothesis-sentence labels and undocumented provenance ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/knowledgator/gliclass-v2.0)).

UTCD's labels, despite the name, are descriptive names rather than descriptions ([UTCD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/claritylab/utcd)). Super-NaturalInstructions has per-task definitions for about 412 classification-type tasks, but only some of them explain individual outputs ([Super-NI tasks](https://github.com/allenai/natural-instructions/blob/master/tasks/README.md)).

## Descriptions pay off only when the reader trains on them

**The clearest pattern in the literature is that descriptions help readers trained to use them and hurt readers that are not.** Yin et al. gave untrained NLI models WordNet definitions instead of label names. Topic accuracy fell from **48.4 with names to 26.0 with definitions alone**, and name-plus-definition helped only on some tasks and NLI training sets ([Yin et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.00161)). Gao, Ghosh and Gimpel fine-tuned RoBERTa on nothing but a few hundred label-description strings, with no task inputs, and raised average accuracy from **54.7 to 72.2 (base) and 58.8 to 77.7 (large)**. The large model then beat text-davinci-003 on five datasets ([Gao et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02239)). Part of that gain is not semantic. Training on the same data with random verbalizers still reached 65.9 and 73.4. That is why a mismatched- or random-description control belongs in any ablation. WaffleCLIP made the same point in vision: random words recovered "comparable performance gains" to LLM descriptors ([Roth et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07282)).

The dialogue results show where descriptions earn their keep: unseen labels. D3ST replaced schema names with descriptions in a T5 model. On SGD, whose test set contains unseen services, JGA rose from **73.7 to 80.0 (Large) and 79.7 to 86.4 (XXL)**. On MultiWOZ, whose schemas are seen in training, names and descriptions tied ([Zhao et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.08904)). Wording robustness is the other payoff and the other risk. Swapping in SGD-X's crowd paraphrases cut JGA by **12-18% relative** for models trained on one wording. Back-translation augmentation of schemas recovered 8-11% and reduced schema sensitivity by 16-17% ([Lee et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.06800)). Description-trained D3ST was less sensitive than name-trained D3ST (0.26 versus 0.37) ([Zhao et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.08904)). Chu et al. showed that wording matters even with names alone. Six plausible AG News label-name sets spanned **49.9 to 71.6 accuracy** in a dual encoder ([Chu et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.04194)).

**For intents specifically, the description's contrastiveness matters more than how much description text there is.** Hong et al. fine-tuned FLAN-T5 3B on 75 CLINC intents, formatted options as "o: d", and tested on 75 unseen-domain intents. A model trained without descriptions scored **84.28 with names and 90.55 when given "dependent" descriptions**, which an LLM wrote with every sibling intent in view. Descriptions written one intent at a time ("independent") barely moved it (84.15). Training with dependent descriptions pushed the score to 91.75, and training with descriptions but testing without them cost 2.2-2.4 points. Five alternating descriptions per intent did no better than one cleaned one (82.05 versus 83.40) ([Hong et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sigdial-1.39.pdf)). Hong also found that an off-the-shelf mpnet ranker over descriptions can prune to the top 10-15 options with little loss: CLINC goes from 44% at k=1 to about 90% at k≈13, and input length falls by about 75% ([Hong et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sigdial-1.39.pdf)). That two-stage pattern suits a late-interaction scorer with cached option encodings. In the bi-encoder setting, Hu et al. report BGE-Large at **71.53 with descriptions versus 62.91 with tokenized names**, about +11 points averaged across models, with variance across models cut from 5.65 to 1.98. ATIS showed no gain ([Hu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17862)). A 2025 preprint reports zero-shot Banking77 rising from 81.6 to 88.8 with LLM-written descriptions. That figure comes from a search snippet that was not fully read, and the same authors warn that naive descriptions degrade on high-cardinality tasks ([Walshe et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12332)). Test-time ensembling over paraphrased descriptions also helps: Gen-Z's gains plateau at **6-10 descriptions per label** ([Kumar et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.07115)).

Training-side regularisers from instruction-tuned information-extraction (IE) models carry over. In GoLLIE, dropping candidate representatives cut zero-shot F1 from 55.3 to 49.9, and class dropout mattered significantly, while name masking and guideline paraphrasing were not significant ([Sainz et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.03668)). SLIMER's LLM-written definitions raised zero-shot NER F1 from 51.3 to 54.0 and sharply cut run-to-run variance. Overly strict guidelines also cost one tag 14 points ([Zamai et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01272v3)). The gap is equally clear. **No paper reports a controlled names-versus-descriptions ablation for a bi-encoder or late-interaction classifier on Banking77, CLINC150 or HWU64.** Hong's cross-dataset comparison sets 3B-7B generative models against smaller name-based encoders, and the authors acknowledge the confound ([Hong et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sigdial-1.39.pdf)).

## Banking77 and CLINC150 already sit inside most zero-shot mixtures

**Direct contamination is the norm, not the exception, among large zero-shot training sets.** UTCD lists CLINC150 as an in-domain component and Banking77 as an out-of-domain one ([UTCD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/claritylab/utcd)). tasksource's label-NLI set includes 9,002 Banking77 and 10,000 CLINC rows, plus non-commercial rows such as BeaverTails ([sources.yaml](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tasksource/zero-shot-label-nli/raw/main/sources.yaml)). tasksource-jev includes two copies of Banking77, CLINC, MASSIVE and SNIPS ([sources.yaml](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tasksource/tasksource-jev-typed-decisions/raw/main/sources.yaml)). Redwood merges all 77 Banking77 and all 150 CLINC intents with their utterances ([redwood_test.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gxlarson/redwood/main/data/redwood_test.json)). RAFT ships a Banking77 task ([RAFT](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ought/raft)). The training list behind Laurer's non-`-c` zeroshot-v2.0 models includes Banking77 and MASSIVE, although the model card also says "pure zeroshot" evaluations excluded them, which conflicts with that list ([datasets_overview.csv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MoritzLaurer/zeroshot-classifier/main/v1_human_data/datasets_overview.csv); [model card](https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/deberta-v3-large-zeroshot-v2.0)). AutoIntent hosts LLM-augmented Banking77 and CLINC150 utterance sets ([AutoIntent](https://huggingface.co/AutoIntent)). The GLiClass paper uses Banking77 only for evaluation ([arXiv 2508.07662](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07662)). The provenance of its training set is undocumented, so treat it as probably clean but unverified.

| zeroshot-v2.0 (non-`-c`) | Banking77, MASSIVE in training list | Do not cite as a zero-shot B77 baseline; use `-c` |
| AutoIntent descriptions and augmentations; Hu et al. descriptions | Derived from held-out label sets | Evaluation-only (alternate wording) |

**LIDM's current pipeline does not yet hold CLINC150 out.** `src/cls/data/stage1.py` samples UTCD's in-domain `clinc_150` training set and removes only the banking and credit-card intents and "exchange rate". It also keeps UTCD's `sgd` minus Check Balance and Transfer Money, and `slurp`, the source corpus of MASSIVE. Its label-NLI filter drops tasks whose names contain "banking" or "clinc", which covers tasksource's two direct leaks. If CLINC150 is to be a true held-out evaluation, `utcd/clinc_150` has to go entirely. Whether that partial holdout, inherited from M0, was deliberate is a decision for the project, not something these notes resolve.

**Semantic overlap needs its own reporting line, because exact-label filters miss it.** SGD's `Banks_1`/`Banks_2` (CheckBalance, TransferMoney) and `Payment_1` paraphrase Banking77's transfer and balance labels. SGD's Alarm, Calendar, Weather, Music and Flights domains mirror CLINC150 domains ([SGD schema](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research-datasets/dstc8-schema-guided-dialogue/master/train/schema.json)). NLU++'s 48 banking intents, Bitext's retail-banking, wealth-management and mortgage verticals, and MINDS-14's e-banking intents all sit in Banking77's domain. HWU64, MASSIVE and SLURP share CLINC150's assistant intents, though not its utterances ([Bitext](https://huggingface.co/bitext); [MINDS-14](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PolyAI/minds14); [MASSIVE](https://github.com/alexa/massive)). Tool data adds a subtler duplicate. When2Call's "Search for a bus itinerary between two cities on a specific date" closely paraphrases SGD's `FindBus`, which suggests BFCL-derived specs repeat SGD schemas ([When2Call](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/When2Call)). That inference rests on one example. LIDM already tags validation sets `zs/` or `ood/` by exact label match. Extending that tag to near-duplicate "name: description" pairs, using embedding similarity against the training option inventory, would split Banking77 and CLINC150 scores into truly novel and neighbour-assisted labels.

**Description writing is its own leakage channel.** Hong's independent descriptions were prompted with three user queries per intent. SLIMER sampled three examples per entity type. 2026 definition-refinement work rewrites definitions from misclassified examples ([Hong et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sigdial-1.39.pdf); [Zamai et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01272v3); [Rehmat et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27335)). Chu et al.'s oracle label names, picked with test knowledge, lifted AG News from 69.9 to 81.9, which shows how much test-informed wording can inflate scores ([Chu et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.04194)). Gao et al. set the clean protocol. Descriptions never see task inputs, and every hyperparameter is tuned on a separate label set (20NG) that is excluded from evaluation ([Gao et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02239)). Published zero-shot intent numbers also use incompatible splits. The PIE-style setup uses 50/25/27-intent subsets of CLINC/HWU/BANKING, Hong uses a 75/75 CLINC domain split, and UTCD places CLINC in-domain. Cross-paper comparison is therefore meaningless unless the protocol matches ([UTCD](https://huggingface.co/datasets/claritylab/utcd); [Hong et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sigdial-1.39.pdf)).

## Build the pool from SGD-X, NLU++ and generated contrastive descriptions

**The in-style, description-bearing core is small: SGD/SGD-X and NLU++ hotels, with SGD's bank and payment services and NLU++ banking moved to a flagged neighbour split.** SGD-X's six wordings are the only released way to train and test paraphrase robustness on intents. Each epoch should draw a different variant, keeping v5 or the original wording back for a robustness check, following the paper's back-translation-versus-oracle logic ([Lee et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.06800)). NLU++'s question-form descriptions train the binary "does this label fit" judgment directly. STARv2 action glosses and summarised ABCD subflows add customer-service variety.

The next ring is name-only intent data that needs generated descriptions: Redwood with Banking77 and CLINC removed (about 224 labels, license unclear), the Bitext verticals (about 330 names before deduplication, CDLA-Sharing-1.0 on the main set), MTOP's 117 intents, and HWU64/MASSIVE as a semantic-neighbour validation set for CLINC rather than training data. Combined, this gives several hundred to about 700 distinct fine-grained intents. That is an estimate; cross-dataset deduplication was not computed ([Redwood](https://aclanthology.org/2022.sigdial-1.45); [Bitext](https://huggingface.co/bitext)). Generate these descriptions Hong-style, with the full label inventory in the prompt so each one contrasts its siblings, and keep one curated canonical description per label ([Hong et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sigdial-1.39.pdf)). Use a different LLM, or a different template, from the one that writes the Banking77 test descriptions, so the model cannot win by matching style. SLIMER and GoLLIE both relied on a single generator, which is the overfitting risk this mitigation targets ([Zamai et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01272v3)).

**The outer ring supplies scale and option-count stress rather than in-style signal.** MetaTool, ToolBench and ToolRet train and test choice among hundreds to thousands of described options. ESCO with TechWolf sentences, IPTC with MN-DS, FewRel and GoEmotions give clean, license-friendly definitional text in other registers. The project already uses Laurer's synthetic hypotheses. LIDM's pass-B window truncates each option at 64 tokens. SGD (~8 words), NLU++ questions, IPTC (~99 characters) and the DeepPavlov one-liners fit comfortably. MeSH (~198 characters) and O*NET (~214 characters) fit only after trimming to the first sentence once the name is prepended. CPC definitions and ABCD guidelines need summarising. The character-to-token conversion is approximate.

On the training side, the evidence above supports four measures:

- Always keep the name in the option string, since definitions alone failed in Yin 2019.
- Show descriptions at training time.
- Occasionally mask the name with a placeholder, so the model must read the description.
- Sample distractor label sets per example, which matches GoLLIE's significant class-dropout and candidate effects.

LIDM's option scoring is already order-free, so GoLLIE's class shuffling is moot. On the evaluation side:

- Freeze the Banking77 and CLINC150 descriptions before any test run, and record whether training-split examples informed them.
- Report name-only and "name: description" scores from the same checkpoint.
- Report the spread across at least two independent description wordings. The project's own `banking77_descriptions.json` and the DeepPavlov or Hu et al. sets are ready-made second wordings.
- Add a random-descriptor control and a mismatched-description control, following WaffleCLIP and Gao et al.

## Conclusion

A description-reading intent model will learn most of its skill from text that does not look like banking chat. Only about 150 officially described intents exist in-style (SGD's 88 and NLU++'s 62), and many of them neighbour the held-out sets. The decisive variable is therefore the style gap between training descriptions and the held-out ones, more than the number of training labels. Hong's finding, that the quality of test-time descriptions outweighs training-time variety, and SGD-X's 12-18% paraphrase penalty point the same way. The most informative experiment LIDM can run is a two-wording, name-versus-description ablation on a strictly cleaned Banking77/CLINC150. It would fill the field's main gap: no late-interaction or bi-encoder result of this kind exists.

The usual reading of Banking77 and CLINC150 as "held-out" benchmarks also needs revisiting. Because nearly every public zero-shot mixture, merged intent benchmark and description release already contains them, published "zero-shot" numbers on them are often not zero-shot. Scores from a pipeline that stratifies by label novelty, and that drops UTCD's CLINC150 split, will be lower than the literature's, and they will be the more trustworthy ones.
