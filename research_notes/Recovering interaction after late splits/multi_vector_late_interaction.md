# Multi-vector and token-level late interaction (ColBERT family, poly-encoders, relatives) as a way to keep candidates separately encoded but recover fine-grained context interaction

Scope note: every number below gives backbone, task, metric and size where the source states them. "Gap closed" = (late-interaction − bi-encoder) / (cross-encoder − bi-encoder) on the **same backbone and training data**. I computed it from the cited tables, so it is arithmetic on cited numbers, not a reported figure. LIDM context used for the inferences: ModernBERT-large (395M, 28 layers, 1024-d); pass A = context, pass B = each option alone; a 2-block fusion head (option self-attention + cross-attention to pass-A tokens, scored at a **pooled option vector**); the joint baseline is Laya (all options in one sequence).

## Q1. Poly-encoder: bi vs poly vs cross accuracy, number of codes, speed

### Takeaway
On response selection with BERT-base, poly-encoders close roughly 30–90% of the bi-to-cross gap (absolute gaps are only 1–3 R@1 points), and they keep near-bi-encoder latency: 18–57 ms vs 2.6 s on GPU for 1k candidates. Accuracy saturates at 16–64 codes. On NLI the same architecture closes only about 25% of a gap 6–9 points wide, so poly-encoders suit "pick a plausible response" and do poorly at "check a detail".

Siamese bi-encoder: MNLI 60.2, RTE 53.3, BoolQ 70.5. — [ar5iv 2112.04195](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.04195); venue [ACL Anthology 2022.emnlp-main.59](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.59/)

### Inferences
- The gap closed falls from 65–88% on response selection to about 25% on MNLI (MixEncoder: (77.3−75.2)/(83.7−75.2)). A handful of pooled context codes attended by one pooled candidate vector cannot represent "does token X in the premise contradict token Y in the hypothesis". This matches the LIDM failure profile: small losses on typed decisions, large losses on VitaminC and RACE.
- LIDM's head is structurally an inverted poly-encoder or MixEncoder: one pooled option vector cross-attends to all pass-A tokens. Poly-encoders use only a few context codes, so LIDM is richer on the context side. The two share the bottleneck that matters, though: **the candidate is a single vector at scoring time**.
- Codes saturate at 16–64. Adding more pooled vectors per option in LIDM is unlikely to fix detail-checking unless those vectors are token-level and can interact with context tokens.

### Gaps
- The fetched page does not confirm the poly-encoder's publication venue. ICLR 2020 is the commonly cited venue, but I did not verify it.
- I found no poly-encoder results on classification with label names (for example Banking77), on fact verification, or on multiple-choice QA.

## Q2. ColBERT family (ColBERT, ColBERTv2, PLAID, XTR, CITADEL, COIL, ME-BERT, MUVERA, ColBERTer, ModernBERT ColBERTs): gap to cross-encoders, scoring operator, cost

### Takeaway
On MS MARCO, token-level MaxSim with BERT-base lands within about 1 MRR@10 point of a BERT-base cross-encoder used as a re-ranker (34.9 vs 36.0), at 61 ms vs 10.7 s per query. Under matched distillation it closes about 70% of the dual-encoder-to-cross-encoder gap (LITE paper: DE 35.5, ColBERT 38.3, CE 39.5). A learned MLP scorer over the same similarity matrix (LITE) closes about 95%. Storage is the main cost: about 18–19 KB per passage uncompressed, cut to roughly 20–36 bytes per token vector by residual compression, or to 1,280 bytes per document with MUVERA. A same-backbone reproduction in 2026 found ColBERT 6.8 nDCG points below its cross-encoder, while mid-fusion models were within about 1.5 points.

### Cited Findings

BERT-base cross-encoder 34.7 (Nogueira and Cho) and 36.0 (authors' own training) at 10,700 ms and 97T FLOPs. BERT-large cross-encoder 36.5 at 32,900 ms. End-to-end ColBERT: 36.0 MRR@10, R@1k 96.8, 458 ms. — [ar5iv 2004.12832](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.12832); venue [SIGIR paper PDF](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~matei/papers/2020/sigir_colbert.pdf)
- Storage for 8.8M passages: 286 GiB (128-d, fp32), 154 GiB (128-d, fp16; end-to-end, 36.0), 54 GiB (48-d, 34.4 re-rank), 27 GiB (24-d fp16, 33.9 re-rank). The ablations, given only in a figure, state that a single vector, average-similarity instead of MaxSim, and removing [MASK] augmentation are each "noticeably" worse. — [ar5iv 2004.12832](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.12832)

**ColBERTv2 (Santhanam et al., NAACL 2022; BERT-base)**
- MS MARCO dev MRR@10 39.7, R@50 86.8, R@1k 98.4, vs ColBERT v1 36.0. Single-vector comparisons: TAS-B 34.7, coCondenser 38.2, RocketQAv2 38.8. MS MARCO index: 16 GiB (1-bit) or 25 GiB (2-bit), vs 154 GiB. **PLAID (Santhanam et al. 2022, DOI 10.1145/3511808.3557325)**
- Centroid interaction and centroid pruning cut ColBERTv2 search latency by up to 7× on GPU and 45× on CPU; the paper body gives 2.5–7× GPU and 9–45× CPU. — [PLAID arXiv 2205.09707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09707v1)

**CITADEL and COIL (CITADEL: Waterloo and Meta, arXiv Nov 2022)**

**XTR (Google DeepMind, arXiv Apr 2023; T5 encoders)**
- Trains token retrieval so that scoring uses only retrieved tokens, with similarities imputed for missing tokens. Scoring FLOPs drop by about 4000× (0.09M vs 0.36B per query). **ME-BERT (Luan, Eisenstein, Toutanova, Collins, TACL; arXiv May 2020; BERT, m = 3–8 vectors per document, max-over-vectors)**
- MS MARCO passage dev MRR@10, retrieval: ME-BERT 0.334 vs DE-BERT (768-d) 0.302. Re-ranking: 0.395 vs 0.391. Document task, retrieval: 0.333 vs 0.288. — [ar5iv 2005.00181](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00181)

**MUVERA (Dhulipala et al., arXiv 2405.19504, May 2024; ColBERTv2 vectors)**
- Fixed Dimensional Encodings turn a set of vectors into one vector whose dot product approximates Chamfer/MaxSim similarity. The end-to-end system uses 10,240-d FDEs; product quantization compresses them 32× to 1,280 bytes per document. Latency is about 90% lower than PLAID on average, recall is about 10% higher on average, and MS MARCO recall is within 0.4% of PLAID. — [arXiv html 2405.19504](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19504)

**ColBERTer (Hofstätter et al., arXiv Mar 2022)**

### Inferences
- With matched training, the gap closed scales with how many candidate vectors survive to scoring time and how expressive the operator is: about 0% for 1–2 pooled vectors (Poly and MixEncoder on MS MARCO), about 24% for 3–8 vectors (ME-BERT on NQ), 70–84% for all tokens with MaxSim (ColBERT in LITE and MixEncoder tables), and about 95% for all tokens with a learned scorer (LITE).
- Cost for LIDM-sized options (my arithmetic). A 10-token option at 1024-d fp16 takes about 20 KB; projected to 128-d fp16, 2.5 KB; with ColBERTv2-style 2-bit residuals, about 360 B. For 77 Banking77 labels, all token vectors fit in well under 1 MB even uncompressed. Storage is irrelevant at LIDM's scale; the retrieval-scale compression literature (PLAID, MUVERA, CITADEL) does not bear on the design choice here.
- The MICE reproduction is a warning: plain MaxSim ColBERT trained in-house on a small backbone fell 6.8 nDCG points below its cross-encoder. Mid-fusion designs with real cross-attention (MORES, PreTTR, MICE) were within about 1.5 points.

### Gaps
- The survey by Lin, Nogueira, Yates ("Pretrained Transformers for Text Ranking") was not fetched, so its tabulated comparisons are not included.
- I did not verify the venues of XTR (NeurIPS 2023?), CITADEL (ACL 2023?), MUVERA (NeurIPS 2024?), COIL (NAACL 2021) or ColBERTer (CIKM 2022). COIL numbers come only from CITADEL's table; COIL's own paper was not fetched.
- The ColBERTv2 paper as read does not isolate how much of the 36.0 → 39.7 gain comes from distillation.
- I found no ColBERT-family result on ModernBERT-large specifically.

## Q3. Late interaction (and relatives) used for classification, label matching, NLI, fact verification and multiple-choice QA

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed paper that applies ColBERT-style MaxSim to label matching, NLI, VitaminC/FEVER-style claim verification, or RACE/CommonsenseQA multiple choice. The non-retrieval evidence comes from neighbouring designs. On BERT-base, fully separate encoders lose 6–24 points on MNLI and 8–13 on RTE. Poly-style and light cross-attention variants recover only part of that. Designs that keep the last 3 of 12 layers joint (DeFormer) lose only 1–2 points on RACE, BoolQ and MNLI. Joint-label "uni-encoders" (GLiClass, Uni-Encoder) beat both bi-encoders and per-pair cross-encoders while staying fast as the number of labels grows.

### Cited Findings
- **MixEncoder** (Yang et al., EMNLP 2023; BERT-base; candidates pre-encoded into k context vectors that attend to query tokens at the last 1–3 layers). MNLI accuracy: Cross 83.7, Dual 75.2, Poly-360 77.3, VIRT 78.3, DeFormer 82.0, MixEncoder-a/b/c 77.5/77.8/78.4. ColBERT is marked "×" (not applicable) for MNLI. Ubuntu R1@10: cross 83.1, dual 81.6, ColBERT 82.9, MixEncoder 83.1–83.3. DSTC7 R1@100: cross 66.8, ColBERT 67.2, MixEncoder-b 68.2. MS MARCO MRR: cross 36.0, ColBERT 35.4, DeFormer 35.7, MixEncoder 31.9–32.5. Latency for 100 queries × 1k candidates on GPU: dual 7.2 ms, MixEncoder-a 8.4 ms, ColBERT 27.0 ms, DeFormer 488.7 ms, cross 949.4 ms. Storage: 0.3 GB for dual, poly and MixEncoder vs 8.6 GB for ColBERT. — [ar5iv 2210.05261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.05261)
- MixEncoder's stated limitation: it "does not perform well on MS MARCO" and "falls short of detecting token overlapping since it loses token-level features by pre-encod[ing] candidates into several context embeddings." — [ar5iv 2210.05261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.05261)
- **VIRT** (Li et al., EMNLP 2022; BERT-base). Cross-encoder / Siamese / DeFormer / DiPair / Poly / Sentence-T5 / VIRT: MNLI 84.1 / 60.2 / 71.1 / 71.3 / 74.5 / 75.9 / 78.6. RTE 66.0 / 53.3 / 55.0 / 55.1 / 57.2 / 59.2 / 60.5. BoolQ 74.1 / 70.5 / 70.9 / 71.3 / 70.9 / 72.0 / 73.1. QQP 90.6 / 80.1 / 88.5 / 88.6 / 88.5 / 90.3 / 90.4. VIRT's final layer lets the token states of the two texts attend to each other, then pools them into (u, v, u−v, max). — [ar5iv 2112.04195](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.04195)
- **DeFormer** (Cao et al., arXiv 2005.00697, 2020). Separate encoding in the lower layers, joint in the upper layers; BERT-base with 9 separate and 3 joint layers. RACE 66.3 → 64.5 (−1.8), BoolQ 77.8 → 76.8 (−1.0), SQuAD F1 88.5 → 87.1, MNLI 84.4 → 82.6 (−1.8), QQP 90.5 → 90.3. Speedup 2.2–3.5×, memory −50 to −73%. XLNet-base with 8+4 layers: RACE 70.3 → 68.7, BoolQ 80.4 → 78.8. The drop stays negligible when the split is at low layers and rises sharply near the output layers. — [ar5iv 2005.00697](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00697)
- **Conflicting reproductions of DeFormer on MNLI**: 82.6 in the original paper, 82.0 in the MixEncoder reproduction, 71.1 in the VIRT reproduction. The configurations probably differ, but neither reproduction explains its setup in the sections I read. — [ar5iv 2005.00697](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00697); [ar5iv 2210.05261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.05261); [ar5iv 2112.04195](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.04195)
- **GLiClass** (Stepanov et al., Knowledgator, arXiv 2508.07662, Aug 2025). Variants: uni-encoder (text and all labels in one sequence, the primary model), bi-encoder, fused bi-encoder, encoder-decoder. Scoring is a dot product or MLP over pooled vectors; there is no token-level late-interaction scorer. Zero-shot average score (described as F1): gliclass-large-v3.0 (DeBERTa-v3) 0.7193, base 0.6764, ModernBERT-large variant 0.6197, ModernBERT-base 0.5577. NLI cross-encoders: deberta-v3-large-zeroshot-v2.0 0.6821, base 0.6559. Throughput (A6000, batch 1) from 1 to 128 labels: gliclass-large 19.05 → 17.60 ex/s; deberta-v3-large NLI cross-encoder 16.82 → 0.25 ex/s. The authors report that DeBERTa-based models consistently beat ModernBERT-based ones. — [arXiv html 2508.07662](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07662)
- **Uni-Encoder** (Song et al., Findings of ACL 2023; BERT). Context and all candidates go in one pass under "arrow attention": candidates attend to the context but not to each other, and all candidates share position ids. Ubuntu V2 R10@1: Uni-Encoder 0.859, Poly-360 0.809, BERT cross 0.781 (a weaker cross baseline than Humeau's 82.8). PersonaChat R20@1 0.869 vs BERT-CRA 0.843. Ablation on Ubuntu V2 R10@1: shared positions 0.859, unique positions 0.837, **candidates allowed to attend to each other 0.826**. About 4× faster than the cross-encoder. — [ar5iv 2106.01263](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01263); venue [ACL Anthology 2023.findings-acl.388](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.388.pdf)
- **ColBERT-style label scoring**: only community artefacts surfaced. One Hugging Face model card claims MaxSim zero-shot classification with "recall 0.9686, F1 0.9081" but names no benchmark. A LinkedIn post ("TokenAlign", Mean-MaxSim over label descriptions) claims 77.96% on 15 held-out Banking77 categories, and its author calls it not a benchmark. Both are unverified and low-quality. — [HF polodealvarado/late_interaction](https://hf-767.pages.dev/polodealvarado/late_interaction); [LinkedIn](https://fr.linkedin.com/in/bilelle-triki)
- **BEIR fact-verification retrieval**, a retrieval proxy rather than claim verification. FEVER nDCG@10: ColBERT 77.1, ColBERTv2 78.5, TAS-B 70.0. Climate-FEVER: 18.4 / 17.6 / 22.8 ([ar5iv 2112.01488](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01488)). Under LITE's matched training: FEVER ColBERT 0.782, LITE 0.788, CE 0.804; Climate-FEVER 0.190 / 0.213 / 0.232; ArguAna 0.433 / 0.424 / 0.519 ([arXiv html 2406.17968](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17968v1)).

### Inferences
- The closest published analogue to LIDM's current head is MixEncoder-a: a pooled candidate vector that attends to query tokens in the last layer. It loses 6.2 MNLI points to the cross-encoder on BERT-base, about the size of LIDM's −7 on VitaminC. The authors attribute this to losing token-level features of the candidate. That points to keeping option **token** states, not a pooled option vector, at scoring time.
- DeFormer's RACE result (−1.8 with 3 of 12 layers joint) suggests LIDM's RACE −5 comes mostly from having no joint layers at all, with only a small fusion head. Giving back about a quarter of the depth as joint or cross-attention layers is the measured way to recover most of it. On ModernBERT-large that would be roughly layers 21–28 (my extrapolation).
- GLiClass and Uni-Encoder suggest that for many short labels (Banking77 −11), the strongest cheap design is joint encoding of all labels with the input, which is Laya-like. The Uni-Encoder ablation (0.826 vs 0.859) further suggests that **blocking option-to-option attention** and sharing option position ids may help. This bears on both Laya and LIDM's option self-attention block, untested here.
- GLiClass's observation that ModernBERT underperforms DeBERTa-v3 as a joint label/text encoder is a backbone caveat for LIDM. It is one group's report.

### Gaps
- I found no measured late-interaction (MaxSim) results on VitaminC, FEVER claim classification, SNLI/MNLI, RACE, CommonsenseQA or Banking77 in peer-reviewed work. The searches returned only retrieval papers.
- I did not obtain GLiClass's per-dataset tables (for example Banking77), nor any bi-encoder-variant numbers from that paper.
- Absolute Uni-Encoder latency numbers appear only in a figure.

## Q4. Does MaxSim handle negation and single-word evidence flips, or is learned cross-attention required? What does late interaction miss?

### Takeaway
MaxSim does not handle negation out of the box. On NevIR, where two documents differ only by negation, ColBERTv1 and ColBERTv2 reach 19.7% and 13.0% paired accuracy, below the 25% random baseline. Bi-encoders reach 6.8–11.1% and cross-encoders 22–51%. The authors trace this to MaxSim never selecting negation tokens as the max for any query token. A 2025 reproduction found that only cross-encoders and listwise LLM re-rankers generalize across negation benchmarks. Analysis papers show ColBERT leans on exact matches of high-IDF terms, which is the opposite of what a single-word evidence flip needs.

### Cited Findings
- **NevIR** (Weller, Lawrie, Van Durme, JHU; EACL 2024; arXiv 2305.07614). Paired accuracy, random = 25%, humans 100% on a 10-instance sample. TF-IDF 2.0%. SPLADEv2 8.0–8.7%. ColBERTv1 19.7% and ColBERTv2 13.0% (110M). Bi-encoders 6.8–11.1% (DPR 6.8, coCondenser 7.7, RocketQAv2 7.8, multi-qa-mpnet 11.1). Cross-encoders: RocketQAv2-CE 22.4, stsb-roberta-large 24.9, MonoT5-small 27.7, nli-deberta-v3-base 30.2, qnli-electra-base 34.1, MonoT5-base 34.9, MonoT5-large 45.8, MonoT5-3B 50.6. — [arXiv html 2305.07614v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07614v2); venue [ACL Anthology 2024.eacl-long.139](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.139.pdf)
- NevIR mechanism quote: "the MaxSim operator in ColBERTv1 ignores negation words, not selecting them as the max for any query token". Fine-tuning can change this so that MaxSim "correctly identifies them". — [arXiv html 2305.07614v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07614v2)
- NevIR fine-tuning (20 epochs on the NevIR train set): all architectures improve but stay far from human. MonoT5 learns fastest, then overfits and loses MS MARCO quality. ColBERT learns more slowly over nearly 20 epochs. Exact numbers appear only in a figure. — [arXiv html 2305.07614v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07614v2)
- **ExcluIR** (Zhang et al., AAAI 2025; 3,452 exclusionary queries built on HotpotQA, 70,293 training queries): late-interaction models "like ColBERT have obvious limitations", and fine-tuning helps but stays "far from satisfactory". I did not retrieve exact scores. — [arXiv html 2404.17288](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17288v1); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33451)
- **Reproduction study** (van den Elsen et al., SIGIR 2025, arXiv 2502.13506): only cross-encoders and listwise LLM re-rankers do reasonably on both negation tasks, and only the cross-encoder category generalizes between the two negation datasets. The paper also criticizes NevIR's pairwise metric. — [arXiv html 2502.13506](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13506v3)
- **A White Box Analysis of ColBERT** (Formal, Piwowarski, Clinchant; arXiv 2012.09650, ECIR 2021). ColBERT captures term importance, which correlates moderately with IDF and less so for IDF > 8. It relies on exact matches for important terms. Stopwords such as "the" and "of" match document tokens that belong to other query terms. — [ar5iv 2012.09650](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.09650); [NAVER Labs blog](https://europe.naverlabs.com/blog/a-white-box-analysis-of-colbert/)
- A 2024 analysis of matching and token pruning found that ColBERT and COIL behave like traditional retrieval models, focusing on important words and exact matches. — [arXiv 2403.13291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13291)
- **XTR token analysis**: for the query token "usual", 100% of T5-ColBERT's top-100 retrieved document tokens are lexically identical to it, vs 8% for XTR. — [ar5iv 2304.01982](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.01982)
- **Theory (LITE)**: without positional encodings, ColBERT's sum-of-max can only represent permutation-equivariant scoring functions. Dot-product dual encoders have a dimension-dependent approximation lower bound. A learned MLP scorer over the similarity matrix is a universal approximator. — [arXiv html 2406.17968](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17968v1)
- **Learned scorer beats MaxSim most where the task resembles argument or claim matching** (LITE vs ColBERT, same backbone, BEIR nDCG@10): Quora 0.839 vs 0.767, Climate-FEVER 0.213 vs 0.190, Touché 0.305 vs 0.274. On ArguAna, LITE scores 0.424, below ColBERT's 0.433, and both trail the CE at 0.519. Taking the top-k aligned tokens in MaxSim instead of the top-1 does not help (k = 1/2/4/8: 0.383/0.378/0.380/0.382). — [arXiv html 2406.17968](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17968v1)

### Inferences
- Single-token flips such as negation ("not"), quantity changes or antonyms (VitaminC's contrastive edits) are the case MaxSim is built to miss. Each context token is rewarded for finding *some* similar option token, and the score is a sum, so a mismatch on one token is outvoted by many matches. MaxSim also has no way to *penalise* an unmatched contradicting token. Learned cross-attention, or a learned scorer over the similarity matrix, can produce negative evidence.
- For LIDM's VitaminC and RACE losses, these results argue against swapping the fusion head for plain MaxSim. The options with measured support are: keep token-level option states and add learned cross-attention between option tokens and context tokens, as in MORES, MICE or DeFormer-style upper layers (Q5); or at minimum use a learned scorer over the option × context similarity matrix (LITE).

### Gaps
- I found no contrast-set or VitaminC-style evaluation of any late-interaction or multi-vector model on claim *classification*. All negation evidence is retrieval-ranking (NevIR, ExcluIR).
- I found no published per-token attribution study that compares what MaxSim and learned cross-attention attend to on contradiction pairs.

## Q5. Hybrid scorers: light learned cross-attention over separately encoded token vectors, and their measured gains

### Takeaway
The best-measured hybrids keep candidate (document) token states and add 2–4 learned cross-attention blocks, or re-use the upper backbone layers as cross-attention. MORES (2 blocks) reaches 98% of BERT's MRR@10. MICE (2026) loses about 1–2 nDCG in-domain and *beats* its cross-encoder out of domain (BEIR 49.7–50.5 vs 44.0 on MiniLM) at 2.5× fewer FLOPs. Initialising the cross-attention from the backbone's self-attention weights is critical: random initialisation drops MORES from 0.3456 to 0.2723 MRR@10. Pooled-candidate hybrids (MixEncoder, VIRT) help NLI far less.

### Cited Findings
- **MORES** (Gao, Dai, Callan, CMU; arXiv 2004.13313, 2020). Document and query representation modules (BERT) run independently; the interaction module stacks blocks of query→document cross-attention, query self-attention and FFN, with document states fixed. MS MARCO dev MRR@10 (re-ranking BM25 top-1000): BERT ranker 0.3527; MORES 1 IB 0.3334, 2 IB 0.3456, 3 IB 0.3423, 4 IB 0.3307. TREC DL19 nDCG@10: BERT 0.7032, MORES 2 IB 0.7026. 2 IB is non-inferior at a 2% margin, and adding 3–4 blocks does not help. — [ar5iv 2004.13313](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.13313)
- MORES initialisation (2 IB): copying BERT self-attention weights into the cross-attention gives 0.3456 / 0.7026 (MRR / nDCG); random initialisation gives 0.2723 / 0.6059. — [ar5iv 2004.13313](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.13313)
- MORES efficiency (1,000 candidates, query length 16, document length 512): 2 IB is 35× faster on CPU than BERT when document representations are cached, and 124× faster when projected keys and values are also cached. Storage is 6.0 MB (1 IB) or 12.0 MB (2 IB) per 512-token document. — [ar5iv 2004.13313](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.13313)
- **MICE** (Vast, Morand, Mothe, Piwowarski; arXiv 2602.16299, Feb 2026, EMNLP 2026). Query and document are encoded independently up to layer ℓ* (4 of 12 for MiniLM, 6 of 10 for Ettin-32M, a ModernBERT-style model). After that, only query tokens are updated: they cross-attend to frozen document states with no further document self-attention or MLP, and keep their own self-attention, which the authors found critical. The cross-attention is seeded from the backbone's self-attention weights, top layers are dropped, and document→query attention can be removed without loss. An optional lexical exact-match head is available. — [arXiv html 2602.16299](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16299)
- MICE results, nDCG@10 (MS MARCO / DL19 / BEIR). MiniLM-L12: CE 45.0 / 74.0 / 44.0; MICE-ℓ4+3 (2.5× fewer FLOPs) 43.2 / 73.4 / 49.7; MICE-ℓ4+all 43.6 / 73.3 / 50.5; ColBERT 38.2 / 69.1 / 40.6; MORES 43.5 / 72.0 / 48.6; PreTTR 43.8 / 71.8 / 46.5. Ettin-32M: CE 43.8 / 71.5 / 48.4; MICE-ℓ3+4 42.7 / 70.5 / 47.9. Latency (MiniLM, 512 document tokens, batch 128, TITAN V): MICE with precomputed documents 113 ms vs ColBERT 130 ms vs CE 470 ms. MICE's peak memory is about 1.8× ColBERT's. Combining MICE with PLAID compression caused a large drop. — [arXiv html 2602.16299](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16299)
- **LITE** (learned separable row/column MLP over the query × document similarity matrix S of size 30×200): closes about 95% of the DE-to-CE gap on MS MARCO (0.393 vs CE 0.395 vs ColBERT 0.383), and beats ColBERT on 11 of 14 BEIR datasets. With frozen encoders, ColBERT reaches 0.112 MRR@10 and LITE with only its scorer trained reaches 0.188. — [arXiv html 2406.17968](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17968v1)
- **DeFormer / PreTTR-style upper-layer fusion**: 3 joint upper layers out of 12 give RACE −1.8, BoolQ −1.0, MNLI −1.8 at 2.2–3.5× speedup (Q3). In MixEncoder's benchmark, DeFormer is the strongest hybrid on MNLI (82.0 vs cross 83.7) but only 1.9× faster and needs 52.7 GB. — [ar5iv 2005.00697](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00697); [ar5iv 2210.05261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.05261)
- **Pooled-candidate light cross-attention**: MixEncoder (k = 1–2 candidate vectors attending to query tokens in the last 1–3 layers) reaches MNLI 77.5–78.4 vs cross 83.7 and dual 75.2 at 85–113× speedup. VIRT (cross-attention between token states at the last layer plus attention-map distillation) reaches MNLI 78.6 vs cross 84.1 and RTE 60.5 vs 66.0. — [ar5iv 2210.05261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.05261); [ar5iv 2112.04195](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.04195)
- **Uni-Encoder** (all candidates with the context in one pass, arrow mask): Ubuntu V2 R10@1 0.859 vs Poly-360 0.809, about 4× faster than the cross-encoder (Q3). — [ar5iv 2106.01263](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01263)
- **jina-reranker-v3** (Wang, Li, Xiao; arXiv 2509.25085, Sep 2025; 0.6B). "Last but not late" interaction puts the query and all candidates in one causal context and reads each document's embedding at its final token. It reports 61.94 BEIR nDCG@10, described as state of the art for its size. — [arXiv 2509.25085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25085)
- **ColBERTer**: a learned combination of a CLS vector and token vectors (0.376 MRR@10 at dim 8) beats either alone (0.326 CLS, 0.131 tokens). — [ar5iv 2203.13088](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.13088)

### Inferences
- LIDM's head differs from MORES and MICE in three measurable ways:
  1. The candidate side is pooled before interaction. MORES and MICE keep every token of the side that gets updated.
  2. The head is small and possibly freshly initialised. MORES and MICE re-use backbone attention weights, and MORES shows random initialisation costs about 23% relative MRR.
  3. LIDM adds option-to-option self-attention, which Uni-Encoder's ablation found harmful for response selection (0.826 vs 0.859).

  Each is a cheap, literature-backed change to test.
- A MICE-style LIDM variant on ModernBERT-large:
  - Encode options separately for the first ℓ* layers (MICE used about 33–60% of depth).
  - Then run the remaining layers, or 3–4 of them, with **option tokens** cross-attending to cached pass-A token states while keeping option self-attention.
  - Score from option tokens instead of a pooled vector.

  The cost per option is about (option_len × ctx_len × d) per interaction layer, cheap for short options. Pass A runs once, so the cost profile stays LIDM's. This is my design extrapolation from the cited results, not a measured configuration.
- MICE's out-of-domain gain over its own cross-encoder (+5.7 to +6.5 BEIR nDCG on MiniLM) hints that mid-fusion can generalize better than full joint encoding. That matters for LIDM's "unseen domains" goals, though it did not hold for Ettin-32M (47.2–47.9 vs 48.4).

### Gaps
- I found no hybrid paper that evaluates on VitaminC, RACE, CommonsenseQA or label-name classification. DeFormer, on RACE and BoolQ, is the closest.
- Lychee/KaLM-Reranker (arXiv 2606.22807, 2026; a decoder cross-attending to pre-encoded passages) and EBCAR (arXiv 2510.13329) surfaced in search but were not fetched, so I have no numbers.
- PreTTR's original paper (MacAvaney et al., SIGIR 2020), DC-BERT and DiPair's original papers were not fetched. Their only numbers here come from the VIRT, MixEncoder and MICE reproductions.

## Q6. Distillation from a cross-encoder into late-interaction or bi-encoder students: how much of the gap it closes

### Takeaway
Cross-encoder distillation is the largest single lever for separately encoded students, and it helps late-interaction and learned-scorer students at least as much as single-vector ones. Under the same teacher (ensemble MRR@10 0.399), a 6-layer CE student reaches 0.395, LITE 0.393, ColBERT 0.383 and a dual encoder 0.355. Distillation moves dual encoders more on TREC DL19 (+4.9 nDCG for BERT-dot vs +2.2 for ColBERT with Margin-MSE). On NQ it moves ColBERT more (+6.6 MRR vs +2.1 for DE). For pair classification, distilling attention maps (VIRT) recovers about 4 MNLI points over a poly-encoder but leaves a 5.5-point gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Margin-MSE** (Hofstätter et al., arXiv 2010.02666, Oct 2020). Teachers' MS MARCO dev MRR@10: BERT-base-cat 0.376, BERT-large-cat 0.381, ALBERT-large-cat 0.385, top-3 ensemble 0.399. TREC DL19 nDCG@10 for students with no distillation → single teacher → ensemble: ColBERT 0.722 → 0.738 → 0.744; BERT-base-dot 0.675 → 0.677 → 0.724; DistilBERT-dot 0.670 → 0.704 → 0.712; DistilBERT-cat 0.723 → 0.739 → 0.747; PreTT 0.717 → 0.748 → 0.737; TK 0.652 → 0.669 → 0.666. — [ar5iv 2010.02666](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02666)
- Margin-MSE cost table (DistilBERT models, 1 query × 1,000 cached documents, TITAN RTX): BERT-cat 950 ms and 10.4 GB GPU memory; BERT-dot 23 ms; ColBERT 28 ms, storage ∝ total term occurrences; PreTT 455 ms; TK 14 ms. — [ar5iv 2010.02666](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02666)
- **ColBERTv2**: KL distillation from the 22M MiniLM-L6 cross-encoder (cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2), 64-way tuples and one round of refreshed hard negatives. Together with residual compression this takes MS MARCO MRR@10 from 36.0 (ColBERT v1) to 39.7, and the BEIR average to about 0.500 (CITADEL's table). — [ar5iv 2112.01488](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01488); [ar5iv 2211.10411](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.10411)
- **TAS-B** (dense single vector, balanced topic-aware sampling plus dual-teacher distillation): 34.7 MS MARCO dev MRR@10, BEIR FEVER 70.0, NQ 46.3, ArguAna 42.7 (as tabulated in ColBERTv2). — [ar5iv 2112.01488](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01488)
- Conflict: the ColBERTer extraction lists "TAS-B" at 0.386 dev MRR@10, which disagrees with the commonly reported 0.347. That row is probably a different configuration or a parsing error, so treat 0.347 as canonical. — [ar5iv 2203.13088](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.13088) vs [ar5iv 2112.01488](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01488)
- **CITADEL**: distillation (CITADEL → CITADEL+) lifts MS MARCO MRR@10 from 0.362 to 0.399 and BEIR from 0.486 to 0.493. — [ar5iv 2211.10411](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.10411)
- **LITE loss ablation**, same 6-layer BERT and same T2 teacher (0.399). MS MARCO dev MRR@10, KL vs Margin-MSE: CE student 0.394 / 0.395, DE 0.355 / 0.350, ColBERT 0.383 / 0.378, LITE 0.388 / 0.393. NQ dev MRR@10, plain cross-entropy → KL → Margin-MSE (19 BM25 hard negatives as teacher setting): DE 0.678 → 0.699 → 0.699, ColBERT 0.690 → 0.754 → 0.756, LITE 0.710 → 0.741 → 0.769. — [arXiv html 2406.17968](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17968v1)
- **XTR without distillation** (T5-xxl) beats distilled ColBERTv2 (BERT-base) on BEIR, 52.7 vs 49.9, but the backbones are not comparable. — [ar5iv 2304.01982](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.01982)
- **DeFormer**: knowledge distillation plus layer-wise representation similarity losses toward the joint model are worth SQuAD F1 85.8 → 87.1 (base) and 87.5 → 90.8 (large); the original joint models score 88.5 and 92.3. — [ar5iv 2005.00697](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00697)
- **VIRT**: distilling the cross-encoder's attention maps into Siamese encoders, used only at training time, gives MNLI 78.6 vs poly 74.5 vs cross 84.1. Applied to Sentence-T5: MNLI 75.9 → 77.2, RTE 59.2 → 61.7, QQP 90.3 → 90.8. — [ar5iv 2112.04195](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.04195)

### Inferences
- For LIDM, the measured recipe is to use the joint Laya model (same backbone, same data) as a teacher for the LIDM head, with KL or Margin-MSE over the option set. ColBERTv2, CITADEL+, Margin-MSE and LITE all report gains of 2–7 points from this. DeFormer's layer-wise representation-similarity loss toward the joint model's upper-layer states is a second measured lever (+1.3 to +3.3 F1). This fits the user's setup because Laya already exists.
- Distillation does not remove the architectural ceiling. Under the same teacher, ColBERT stays at 0.383 vs 0.395, and VIRT's NLI student stays 5.5 points below. Distillation should go together with keeping token-level option states and learned cross-attention (Q5), not replace them.

### Gaps
- The Margin-MSE MS MARCO dev MRR@10 for each student was not extracted; only the TREC DL19 numbers above were.
- The ColBERTv2 paper as read has no clean ablation isolating distillation from hard-negative mining.
- I found no cross-encoder-to-late-interaction distillation results on NLI, fact verification or multiple-choice tasks. VIRT and DeFormer are the nearest, both with pooled or upper-layer students.
